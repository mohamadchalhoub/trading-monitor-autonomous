## 4. The two models do NOT agree, and that is the headline

  Over the SAME interval (2026-09-15T01:00:00.517Z -> 2026-09-16T13:58:25.473Z, 1.54 days):
      CLOSED_M1_APPROXIMATION:  1 signals, 1 trades
      TICK_REPLAY (faithful):   39 signals, 11 trades
      ratio:                    39.0x more signals under the faithful model

  Over each model's own full span:
      CLOSED_M1_APPROXIMATION:  0.57 signals/day
      TICK_REPLAY (faithful):   30.75 signals/day

  This gap is expected, and it is the point. One observation per closed bar can
  only see a crossing that survives to the bar boundary; the live engine sees
  every crossing as it happens, including the many that form and reverse inside
  a single minute. The two models therefore trade different opportunities at
  very different rates.

  The practical consequence: the long-horizon approximation numbers above must
  NOT be read as an estimate of live performance, not even a rough one. They
  describe a different strategy. The faithful numbers are the right ones, and
  there are far too few of them to conclude anything from.

## 5. What would make this evaluation stronger

  The only way to evaluate this strategy faithfully over a long period is ordered
  tick data over that period. This deployment currently has ~2 days. Running the
  collector with the live tick stream enabled accumulates it going forward, and
  the same replay above can then be re-run over a genuinely representative span.
  Until then, the approximation is the only long-horizon number available, and it
  is a different strategy from the one that trades.
